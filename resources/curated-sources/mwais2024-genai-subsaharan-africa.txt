ABSTRACT  
AI can improve intelligence and capabilities, paving the door for transformative outcomes in Africa's socioeconomic revolutio n. 
The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) can be a useful framework for investigating the adoption 
of gener ative artificial intelligence (Gen -AI) in Sub -Saharan Africa. UTAUT analyzes a variety of elements that influence 
technology acceptance and use, such as performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating 
conditions. These constru cts can shed light on the drivers and challenges affecting the application of generative AI in Africa. 
To support Jain’s model, a uthors also argue that algorithmic aversion mediates the influence of performance expectancy and 
the use of AI. An online surve y of African -based technology professionals using AI -enabled tools has been distributed and we 
present initial results in this paper. We offer focused techniques to utilize generative AI's revolutionary potential for inn ovation, 
economic growth, and social  development in Africa based on an in -depth examination of the constraints and opportunities.

INTRODUCTION  
Artificial intelligence (AI), particularly machine learning (ML), has made tremendous advances in data a nalysis and computing 
in recent years. These advancements have enabled programs to operate intelligently, demonstrating the ability to grasp and 
effectively process data (Sarker, 2021). Generative AI (Gen -AI) has emerged as a transformational technology wi th the 
potential to disrupt numerous elements of the global economy and African society (Ferrag et al., 2023). Several prominent 
corporations have made substantial contributions to the field of artificial intelligence (AI) research, which has revolutioni zed 
various industries around the world. OpenAI (OpenAI et al., 2021), Google (Lin, 2023), Microsoft (Brühl, 2023), Meta, 
Deepmind (Goldstein et al., 2023), Nvidia (Khalifa et al., 2023), IBM (Townsend & Hunt, 2019), and many others are 
prominent players in creating generative AI.  
OpenAI, a major organization dedicated to ensuring that artificial intelligence benefits all of humanity (ShneidermanBen, 
2020), has been at the forefront of cutting -edge research, pioneering advancements in natural language process ing and 
generative models. Google, a digital behemoth, has made amazing advances in AI research with projects such as Google Brain 
(Sajjadi et al., 2018) and DeepMind (Serrano -Muoz et al., 2022), pushing the boundaries of machine learning and neural 
networ ks. Microsoft (Dao, 2023), another significant competitor, has made significant investments in AI research, employing 
cutting -edge approaches in fields such as computer vision (Chociej et al., 2019) and speech recognition (COA, 2020). These 
firms, along wi th many others, have played an important role in defining the AI landscape and have enormous promise for 
unlocking new opportunities for individuals, enterprises, and governments worldwide (Mhlanga, 2023; Salvaris et al., 2018).  
In Sub -Saharan Africa, the use of AI -enabled tools is evolving at a very slow pace, millions of people are not even aware of 
Generative AI tools ( Floyd , 2023). From the healthcare sector to education, agriculture, and infrastructure, Gen AI tools have 
rapidly become consistent in th e lives of citizens. Although through the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, AI is 
beginning to make some level of impact , the majority of Sub -Saharan African nations currently lack comprehensive regulatory 
frameworks that would effectively enco urage the development and collection of high -quality driven data, govern AI -driven 
business models, and incentivize ethical AI.

fostering innovation and sustainable development; (2) What stimulates the interests of Sub -Saharan African residents and

The reluctance towards adopting AI can stem from its distinctive nature compar ed to other types of technology (Jain et al., 
2022). (Venkatesh, 202 2) emphasizes the importance of identifying unique factors that influence the adoption of AI -based 
technology, given its emerging form. Scholars have been pushed to think distinctly about traditional technology adoption

its effects on users in Sub -Saharan Africa. Extending the UTAUT model will directly or indirectly increase our understand ing 
of this technological and social phenomenon (Makarius et al., 2020)  
UNIFIED THEORY  OF ACCEPTANCE AND U SE OF TECHNOLOGY   
The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) (Williams et al., 2015)  is a wel l-known theoretical 
framework that seeks to comprehend and explain people's acceptance and use of technology. UTAUT was developed by 
Venkatesh et al. (2003) to provide a thorough understanding of technology adoption behavior. It incorporates and expands 
numerous previous theories, including the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA).  
(Cao et al., 2021; Jain et al., 2022) extended the approach by integrating an integrated AI acceptance -avoidance model in their 
study  as see n in Figure 1 . This expanded model considers both positive and negative aspects that influence managers' attitudes 
and behaviors toward the deployment of AI. Previous research has identified technology's detrimental consequences (Vinuesa 
et al., 2020; Flor idi & Cowls, 2022 & Vimalkumar et al., 2021). As a result, when creating a model, it is critical to account the 
unfavorable impressions of AI (Davenport et al., 2020; Wang & Siau, 2019 & Westerlund, 2019).

UTAUT identifies four key factors that influence i ndividuals' acceptance and use of technology:  
Performance Expectancy  
This factor examines how strongly people believe that employing a specific technology would improve their performance and 
help them reach their goals. It emphasizes on the perceived benefits and expected positive results of technology use (Whittaker 
et al., 2018; Ain et al., 2016 & Malik et al., 2019). Although AI has been shown to improve performance expectancies, there i s 
little empirical evidence of the role AI aversion plays in it s adoption (Jain et al., 2022). Owing to these, we propose the following 
hypotheses:  
H1: Performance expectancy will positively affect the use of Gen -AI in Sub -Saharan Africa  
H9: AI aversion will mediate the influence of performance expectancy and the use  of Gen -AI in Sub -Saharan Africa  
Effort Expectancy  
The perceived ease of use and utility of technology is reflected in effort expectancy. It takes into account elements such as  the 
complexity of learning and using technology, as well as individuals' con fidence in their capacity to do so (Rahi et al., 2019;

Social Influence  
Social influence research looks at the impact of social norms, subjective norms, and other people's opinions and 
recommendations on individuals' technology adoption decisions (Manca et al., 2019; Beldad & Hegner, 2018; Hong & Cho, 
2023 ). It investigates the impact of social elements, such as peers, superiors, and influential individuals, on changing people's  
views and intentions toward technology, particularly AI. This led to the hypotheses below:  
H4: The use of Gen -AI tools in Sub -Saha ran Africa  is influenced positively by social influence.  
H6: Social influence influences algorithmic aversion  in Sub -Saharan Africa . 
Facilitating Conditions  
The availability of appropriate resources, support, and infrastructure to assist technology adoption is referred to as facili tating 
conditions (Kumar & Ayedee, 2021 & Grover et al., 2022). It takes into account things like technical support, training, corp orate 
policies, and interoperability with existing systems. In Sub -Saharan Africa, government assistance for information technology 
has a multiplier effect on the use of information technology tools (AbdulKareem et al., 2020; Beda, 2019). Th is assistance has 
taken the shape of facilitating conditions. Today, policies in the form of supportive conditions for AI tools are being 
implemented (Sy et al., 2019). This led to the following hypotheses:  
H3: Facilitating conditions have a positive impact on the usage  behavior of Gen -AI products  in Sub -Saharan Africa .  
H5: Facilitating conditions have an effect on algorithmic aversion  in Sub -Saharan Africa . 
Algorithmic Aversion  
This refers to people's reluctance or skepticism in relying on or accepting algorithmic dec ision -making systems (Hobson et al., 
2023 & Oleksy et al., 2023). It derives from a lack of trust, concerns about openness, and apprehension about potential biase s 
or errors in algorithmic processes (Mahmud et al., 2023). Algorithmic aversion can appear in  a variety of scenarios, including 
the employment of AI tools in hiring processes, loan approvals, and recommendation systems (Bogert et al., 2021& Berger et 
al., 2021).  
Due to perceived limitations or ethical problems linked with algorithmic decision -making, individuals may choose human 
judgment or traditional decision -making methods over algorithms (Cao et al., 2021). The necessity of addressing transparency, 
fairness, and accountability (Hobson et al., 2023) in the design and deployment of algorithmic s ystems (Raji, 2020)  to 
encourage confidence and acceptance (Cabiddu et al., 2022) among u sers is highlighted by algorithmic aversion. Based on this, 
we hypothesize that  
H7: The use of Gen -AI tools is influenced by Algorithmic Aversion  in Sub -Saharan Africa .

contexts, user groups, and technologies. As a result, the framework allows for the incorporation of new moderators (Celik, 
2016) to help explain the adoption behavior.  
UTAUT gives useful insights into the elements influencin g individuals' adoption (Akinnuwesi et al., 2016) and usage of 
technology by taking these four major factors into account. It has been frequently used to assist system design (Williams et al., 
2015), and inform methods for fostering successful technology i mplementation (Maghazei et al., 2022) and utilization (Beaman 
et al., 2021).

RESEARCH DESIGN  
This study proposes the use of the UT AUT model to test the adoption of Gen -AI tools, of course with the addition of algorithmic 
aversion which adds value to the model. We believe that this model will help us understand the current state of Gen -AI tools 
adoption in Sub -Saharan Africa. We are c urrently in the process of data collection from technology professionals  in Nigeria, 
Cameroon,  and Uganda whose responses will allow us to strengthen our proposed model. To the best of our knowledge, this is 
the first time this proposed model is being used  to test the adoption of Gen -AI in sub –Saharan Africa . People play a significant 
role in the acceptance of new digital tools. In future work, we plan to conduct a user survey on what Gen -AI tools are used by 
residents across the different sectors from educ ation, government, business, and healthcare. The survey could also identify 
current challenges faced by residents.

CONCLUSION  
Finally, this study underscores the revolutionary potential of generative AI in Africa, as well as the necessity for concentr ated 
efforts to fully realize its benefits. Africa can use generative AI to drive innovation, economic growth, and societal improv ement 
by tackling issues and executing smart actions. Africa stands to profit from advances in generative AI, and by encouraging 
collaboration, investment, and skill building, we can harness its power for the continent's broader socioeconomic growth.
